# User research write up

##Summary of user research so far
- Usability tested a prototype user journey for Mandatory Drug Testing (MDT) in the Prisons Digital Services style.
- Conducted semi-structured interviews/usability tests with four current MDT staff.
- Generally seen as a large improvement over the old NOMIS process.
- Generally seen as straight forward and feels familiar.
- Most critical comments regard ‘nice-to-haves’ to save time (for example, looking up prisoner information in certain places.)



## What we have done so far

### Context
- Part of wider Move & Improve project to modernise NOMIS, this time with MDT and their monthly process.
- Gathered insights from Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) and current MDT staff to understand their current journey, needs, and pain points.
- Lead to a ‘sprint-in-a-day’ with participants using Github CoPilot to create a prototype Prisons Digital Services (DPS) style journey

### Prototype pre-testing
- From our sprint-in-a-day, we built three prototypes that we have since synthesised into one with the help of our SME and a crit workshop with designers in wider teams.
- Workshopped questions to ask and observations to look for when taking current MDT staff through the prototype.  
### Current study
- Four remote semi-structured 1-hour interviews and usability tests with current MDT staff, gathering and mapping their insights.
- Small changes and tweaks made throughout in the interest of time and testing these changes.



## Insights for each screen in the journey

### Random lists (main list and random list present within tabs, historical and current)
- Columns contain useful reference info: Prisoner, which list they’re from (important to be clear), release date (Calculated Release Date – a rough estimate users expect), when in custody from, last test. Users would also prefer if their day’s activity, location, ethnic code and religion be somewhere for easy reference - however they are not included in the design as they don’t help the user to prioritise which prisoner they are going to test next.
- Progress useful, especially weekend target (if accurate, though one user claims it isn’t a requirement at their prison) and how many reserves activated on reserve tab.
- Other users described no needing an in-progress status as you would fill out in one go post-test.
- Would need a way of undoing a recorded test just in case of mistakes - however we will handle that through a back-end process, not on the UI.
- Generally, also very clear and easy to use. “it appears so much better than the current way the list is produced and easier to organise, I like the idea of the previous months list”, "less jumping through hoops to find certain things. It seems much easier already".
- On the ‘How are the testing lists ordered?’ dropdown: whilst not seen as really needed or necessary for staff themselves, they generally agree it’s good to have down somewhere as long as it’s accurate.
